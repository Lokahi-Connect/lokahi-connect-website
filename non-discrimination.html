Our Commitments

Statement of Non-Discrimination

Lōkahi means unity, harmony, and the weaving together of what is distinct into something whole. Everyone who learns, works, or partners with us has the right to do so free from discrimination, harassment, and retaliation.

Our Commitment

Unity Is the Work

Every learner, family, employee, volunteer, contractor, partner, and community member belongs here — and belongs fully.

Lokahi Connect does not discriminate against, exclude from participation in, deny the benefits of, or subject to harassment any person on the basis of a protected characteristic or identity. This commitment applies to every program, service, employment practice, and business operation we undertake.

We are a Washington-registered nonprofit, and our practices are designed to meet or exceed applicable federal law; Washington State law (including the Washington Law Against Discrimination, RCW Chapter 49.60, and RCW 28A.642 prohibiting discrimination in Washington public education); and all applicable local ordinances.

Who Is Protected

Every Identity, Fully Welcomed

We do not make decisions about admission, enrollment, employment, contracting, or participation on the basis of — among others:

Race, color, ethnicity, ancestry, or national origin
Creed, religion, spiritual practice, or absence of religious belief
Sex, including pregnancy, childbirth, lactation, and related medical conditions
Gender, gender identity, gender expression, and transgender or nonbinary status
Sexual orientation
Age
Marital status and familial status (including families with children)
Honorably discharged veteran or military status
Citizenship or immigration status
Sensory, mental, or physical disability
Neurodivergence and cognitive variability — including autism, ADHD, dyslexia, dyspraxia, dyscalculia, Tourette's, and all other forms of neurological difference
Chronic health conditions, medical condition, genetic information, or perceived health status
HIV/AIDS or Hepatitis C status
The use of a trained dog guide or service animal by a person with a disability
Parental, family, or caregiver status
Primary language, language proficiency, or accent
Socioeconomic status, source of income, or housing status
Status as a victim or survivor of domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking
Political affiliation or political belief (where protected by law)
Any other characteristic protected by federal, Washington State, or local law

This list is not exhaustive. When a person holds multiple identities, we recognize that discrimination and harassment can operate at the intersection of those identities — and we take intersectional harm seriously.

Where This Applies

Every Space We Hold

Our non-discrimination commitments extend to every corner of our work — in person, online, and in partnership.

Programs & Learners

Admission, enrollment, placement, curriculum, instruction, assessment, progression, the Meaning-First Repository, and every other learner-facing decision. Neurodivergent learners are central to our mission, not an afterthought.

Employment & Volunteering

Recruitment, hiring, compensation, benefits, scheduling, training, promotion, discipline, and separation — for full-time, part-time, temporary, contract, volunteer, and board roles alike. We are an equal opportunity employer.

Partnerships & Community

Vendor selection, contracting, funder relationships, communications, digital platforms, events, and physical spaces. We expect our partners to uphold nondiscrimination standards consistent with this statement.

Accessibility

Access Is Foundational

Our commitment to neurodivergent and disabled participants is foundational — not an add-on. We design for access from the start, and we welcome requests for whatever additional support makes participation possible.

Reasonable Accommodations

We provide reasonable accommodations to students, employees, applicants, volunteers, and participants — including accommodations for disability, neurodivergence, pregnancy and related conditions, religious practice, and language access — unless doing so would impose an undue hardship. We engage in good-faith, interactive conversation to find what works, and we will not retaliate against anyone for requesting an accommodation or asserting a disability-related right.

Trained dog guides and service animals are welcome, as required under the ADA and Washington law (RCW 49.60.218).

Our digital platforms, print materials, and physical spaces are designed to meet or exceed applicable accessibility standards (including, where applicable, WCAG 2.1 AA for digital content). Our learner-facing materials apply our INT-informed design principles — reducing cognitive load, supporting regulation, preserving learner agency, and maintaining assumed competence.

To request an accommodation or language assistance: contact us at stephanie@lokahiconnect.org or (808) 280-4368. Requests may be made verbally or in writing, in any language or format that works for you.

Harassment & Retaliation

Zero Tolerance, Full Stop.

Harassment on the basis of any protected characteristic is a form of discrimination, and it is strictly prohibited. This includes slurs, stereotyping, unwelcome jokes or images, unwelcome physical contact, intimidation, bullying or cyberbullying, and sexual harassment — whether in person, online, on personal devices, or outside normal hours, wherever the conduct affects a person's ability to participate in or benefit from our programs, workplace, or community.

It also includes deliberate misgendering, deadnaming, or refusal to use a person's stated name or pronouns, and deficit-framing, mockery, or exclusionary behavior directed at neurodivergent or disabled individuals.

We do not tolerate retaliation. No one who reports a concern in good faith, participates in an investigation, requests an accommodation, advocates for their own or another person's protected rights, or declines to participate in conduct they reasonably believe violates this policy will face retaliation. Retaliation is itself a violation of this policy and of Washington law.

Reporting a Concern

We Want to Hear From You

You do not need to be certain that a violation occurred in order to raise a concern. Reports may be made by the affected person or by a witness, family member, or third party — in any language or communication format, with support from a trusted person if that helps.

Email

Reach our Policy Contact directly.

stephanie@lokahiconnect.org

Phone or Text

Speak with a person, leave a message, or text.

(808) 280-4368

In Person or Video

Contact us to schedule a conversation in whatever format works best for you. Accommodations for the reporting process itself are available on request.

We acknowledge receipt of reports promptly (typically within three business days), assess whether immediate protective measures are needed, and conduct a prompt, thorough, impartial, and trauma-informed inquiry. To the extent possible and permitted by law, reports are handled with discretion and shared only with those who need to know.

If your concern involves our Policy Contact, you may direct your report to the Board of Directors via info@lokahiconnect.org.
