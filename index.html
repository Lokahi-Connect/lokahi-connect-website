Individualized support for reading, spelling, writing, and comprehension

Learning support begins with your interests, questions, and goals. Lokahi Connect offers personalized online support for reading, spelling, writing, comprehension, and learning strategies for children, teens, and adults, including people with dyslexia. With roots in Maui, Hawaiʻi, and Bainbridge Island, Washington, we welcome learners from these communities and beyond. Start with a free 30-minute online consultation.

Making connections

Discover how words work together

Reading and writing bring together spelling, pronunciation, meaning, and context. We help learners explore those connections and use what they discover in everyday reading and writing.

🔤

Explore a question

A question about a word, a sentence, or an idea can open an investigation. We connect explicit teaching and practice with what the learner wants to understand.

📉

Notice connections

Compare related words, notice how their spelling and pronunciation connect, and explore what they mean in different contexts.

🧩

Use what you learn

Try an idea with another word, a new text, or a message you want to write. Together, we notice what is useful and what needs more support.

Foundations of our practice

How Lokahi Connect Teaches

Structured Word Inquiry (SWI)

We investigate how words are built and used, compare evidence, and connect discoveries to reading and writing. Questions can lead from a word to a sentence or a whole text.

Peter N. Bowers is the intellectual source of SWI and the Four Questions. Bowers and Kirby (2010) introduced the term Structured Word Inquiry in a Grade 4/5 morphological vocabulary intervention.

Support for different ways of learning

Our approach to learning design uses visible choices, manageable tasks, and flexible pacing. We adapt the support while keeping ideas accurate and meaningful.

Mediated learning in practice

The educator and learner explore together. We invite questions, explain ideas, notice useful strategies, and consider where else they might help.

What we measure

Notice progress together

We review work, listen to the learner’s perspective, and look at how ideas are used in new situations. Assessments may also help guide instruction. Together, we use this information to adjust goals and support.
