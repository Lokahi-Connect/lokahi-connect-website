Research-informed

The thinking behind the Lokahi WORD Project

We help learners explore how meaning, word parts, spelling, pronunciation, grammar, and word history work together. Questions and discoveries connect to a practical purpose: understanding a text, spelling a word, or expressing an idea.

Foundations of our practice

How we bring teaching and inquiry together

Structured Literacy guides what we teach and how we explain and practice it. Structured Word Inquiry helps us investigate language through questions and evidence. Our design approach adapts tasks and supports to the learner, while mediated learning emphasizes the relationship between educator and learner. Together, these foundations connect explicit teaching, inquiry, and meaningful practice.

Structured Word Inquiry (SWI)

Structured Word Inquiry invites learners to ask questions, compare examples, and test ideas about written language. An investigation may focus on one word or extend into phrases, sentences, and whole texts. We explore meaning, word structure, related words, and the roles of letters in spelling and pronunciation.

Four questions to explore and revisit

  1. What does it mean?
  2. How is it built?
  3. What other words are related?
  4. How do the letters work here, including how they represent pronunciation?
Word sums — the play family

<re> + <play> → replay

<play> + <ful> → playful

A word sum shows the written parts that combine to form a word. Angle brackets mark spelling, and the arrow leads to the completed word.

In “We replay the game,” the prefix <re> adds the idea of playing again. In “a playful game,” the suffix <ful> helps form a word that describes the game. Both words keep the base <play>, while the added part and the sentence shape the meaning.

What stays connected? What changes? How does the word work in each sentence?

At Lokahi Connect, we use these four question areas flexibly, moving between them as the learner’s questions and the evidence suggest. A spelling, pronunciation, meaning, or sentence question can be the starting point. Peter N. Bowers is the intellectual source of SWI and the Four Questions. Bowers and Kirby (2010) introduced the term Structured Word Inquiry in a Grade 4/5 morphological vocabulary intervention.

Designing for different ways of learning

Integrative Neurodevelopmental Theory (INT) is Lokahi Connect’s internal framework for designing learning supports. It informs choices such as visible directions, flexible pacing, manageable tasks, and different ways to respond. We adapt these supports to the person and the activity.

Mediated learning in practice

Mediated Learning Experience, associated with Reuven Feuerstein, emphasizes purposeful interaction between educator and learner. We explore ideas together, listen to each other, and connect discoveries to situations where they may be useful.

Key focus areas:

A shared purpose Meaning Two-way participation Connections to other situations Recognizing capability Pacing and regulation
Room to explore and revisit

Evidence guides the path through an investigation

These are areas we can explore in any order. The learner’s questions, the language being studied, and the purpose guide where we begin and what we revisit.

Possible focus

Meaning in context

Explore what a word means in a sentence, image, text, or everyday situation.

Possible focus

Structure

Investigate the base and any prefixes or suffixes. Use word sums to show how these parts combine.

Possible focus

Related words

Compare possible word relatives and look for evidence of shared structure and meaning.

Possible focus

Spelling and pronunciation

Explore graphemes—the letters or groups of letters working as spelling units—and their roles in the word, including how they represent pronunciation.

Possible focus

Using and reflecting on learning

Try an idea with another word or situation. Notice what carries over, what changes, and which supports are useful.
