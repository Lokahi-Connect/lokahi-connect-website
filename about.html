About Lokahi Connect

Connecting literacy inquiry, evidence, and community

Lokahi Connect is a nonprofit developing student learning resources, educator learning, and open online tools that make written language more coherent and more learnable.

Leadership and history

Experience rooted in learners, families, and community

Founder and Executive Director

Stephanie Steinshouer

Stephanie Steinshouer is the Founder and Executive Director of Lokahi Connect and an educational therapist based on Bainbridge Island. She holds CERI and NILD certifications and serves on the Board of Directors for the Washington State Branch of the International Dyslexia Association. Her work focuses on literacy support, educator learning, family engagement, and expanding access to comprehensive dyslexia-informed practices.

Our story

From direct support to broader access

Founded by Stephanie Steinshouer, Lokahi Connect grew from a commitment to supporting learners and families through more coherent, evidence-informed literacy learning. Today, the 501(c)(3) nonprofit develops student learning resources, educator learning, and open online tools, with local roots in Bainbridge Island, Washington, and Maui, Hawaiʻi, and broader access online.

What we work toward

Written language should become more understandable, not more mysterious

Lokahi Connect helps students, families, and educators investigate how written language preserves relationships among meaning, morphology, grammar, spelling, history, and context. No single dimension is treated as the whole system.

Student learning

Resources and possible instructional formats are designed to support reasoning, transfer, and meaningful access rather than memorization alone.

Educator learning

Professional learning and coaching may help educators mediate inquiry, document evidence, and preserve linguistic accuracy.

Open resources

Free online tools make structured word investigation available to students, families, and educators beyond a single location.

How we work

Dignity, evidence, and honest boundaries

Preserve learner agency

Learners are treated as capable meaning-makers. Support should expand participation and understanding without lowering expectations.

Use evidence carefully

Student responses and completed tasks are bounded performance evidence. No single answer, speed, modality, or task proves understanding.

Name uncertainty

When linguistic, instructional, access, or implementation evidence is incomplete, questions remain open rather than becoming unsupported conclusions.
