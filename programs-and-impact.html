Student learning

Designed for students

Individualized Lokahi WORD Project instruction may be offered in structured, inquiry-based formats paced to support transfer rather than memorization. Formats and availability vary; contact us for current information.

Designed for: learners who need more coherent spelling instruction or benefit from explicit, structure-based teaching.

Educator Professional Development

For Educators

Professional learning and coaching may be offered for teachers, reading specialists, special educators, and speech-language pathologists. The focus is how to mediate inquiry, not just how to deliver a script. Formats and availability vary; contact us for current information.

Designed for: K-12 teachers, reading specialists, special educators, and SLPs.

Digital Resources

For students, families, and educators

The Lokahi WORD Project Student Lab is a free, web-based tool for structured word investigation. It includes read-aloud support, adjustable speech controls, and scaffolded activities that support learner-controlled pacing.

Who can use it: Students, families, and educators.

What we measure

Progress is documented through multiple kinds of evidence

We look at student work, word-family transfer, standardized assessments where appropriate, session records, and educator implementation evidence. We publish individual case stories carefully: in de-identified form and with enough context to explain what was measured.

Measurement focus

Student work, explanations, selections, and completed investigations are bounded performance evidence. Inferences about current understanding require the task, supports, context, and plausible alternatives to be documented.

Student reasoning1Can the learner explain what the word means, how it is built, and why its spelling makes sense?
Transfer2Can the learner apply the same reasoning to a new or related word?
Implementation3Can an educator use the routine accurately and consistently in practice?

Individual case stories are shared only with de-identification and enough methodological context to explain what was measured.

How investigation works

The Four Questions support flexible, recursive inquiry

These areas can be revisited in any direction. Entry point and movement depend on the word, construction, context, learner observation, and purpose—not a mandatory sequence.
